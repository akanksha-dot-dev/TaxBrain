## HRA: Old Regime Only

House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption is available **only in the Old Regime**. If you're on the New Regime, your HRA is fully taxable — but that's okay because the lower slab rates usually compensate.

## The Min-of-3 Formula

HRA exemption = **minimum** of:

1. **Actual HRA received** from employer
2. **50% of Basic** (metro cities) or **40% of Basic** (non-metro cities)
3. **Rent paid minus 10% of Basic salary**

### What Counts as Metro?

For HRA purposes, only **4 cities** are classified as metro:
- Mumbai
- Delhi
- Kolkata
- Chennai

> **Common trap:** Gurugram, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, and Noida are **NOT metro** for HRA. They get the 40% rate, not 50%.

## Example: Gurugram (Non-Metro)

```
Basic salary: ₹91,667/month
HRA received: ₹45,833/month (50% of Basic in CTC)
Rent paid: ₹13,000/month

Exemption = minimum of:
  1. HRA received:       ₹45,833
  2. 40% of Basic:       ₹36,667  (non-metro)
  3. Rent - 10% of Basic: ₹3,833  (₹13,000 - ₹9,167)

HRA Exemption = ₹3,833/month = ₹46,000/year
```

Notice how low rent (₹13,000) makes option 3 the smallest, severely limiting the exemption. This is why HRA exemption is most valuable for **high-rent** payers.

## When Is HRA Worth Choosing Old Regime?

HRA alone rarely makes old regime better. You need HRA **plus** other deductions:

| Monthly Rent | Annual HRA Exemption (approx) | Old Regime Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| ₹10,000 | ₹10,000 | Minimal |
| ₹25,000 | ₹1,54,000 | Moderate |
| ₹40,000 | ₹3,34,000 | Significant |
| ₹60,000+ | ₹5,14,000+ | Old regime likely wins |

## Pro Tips

1. **Get a rent receipt** — Even for PG/shared accommodation. Keep receipts with landlord's PAN if rent > ₹1L/year
2. **Paying rent to parents?** — Valid if parents own the property. Parents declare rental income, you claim HRA exemption
3. **No HRA in salary structure?** — You can still claim Section 80GG (up to ₹5,000/month) if you pay rent but don't receive HRA
