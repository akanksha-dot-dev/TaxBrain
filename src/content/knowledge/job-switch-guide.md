## Why Job Switching Creates Tax Complexity

When you switch jobs mid-year, you have income from **two employers**. Each employer calculates TDS independently based on their portion of your salary. This often leads to:

1. **Under-deduction of TDS** — each employer assumes their salary is your only income
2. **Wrong slab application** — each employer puts you in a lower slab than your combined income warrants
3. **Surprise tax bill** when filing ITR

## The Form 12B Problem (Now Form 124)

Form 12B (renamed to **Form 124** under Income Tax Act 2025) is how you inform your new employer about income from your previous job. **This is the most important document for job switchers.**

### What Form 12B/124 Does

When you submit Form 12B to your new employer:
1. They **add your previous salary** to their salary for TDS calculation
2. They **account for TDS already deducted** by your previous employer
3. Your monthly TDS at the new job becomes **higher** but **correct**

### What Happens If You Don't Submit It

| Without Form 12B | With Form 12B |
|---|---|
| New employer deducts TDS on ₹15L only | New employer deducts TDS on ₹19L combined |
| Monthly TDS: ~₹10,000 | Monthly TDS: ~₹17,000 |
| ₹56,000 shortfall at ITR filing | Zero shortfall |
| Possible interest under Section 234B/234C | Clean filing |

> **Bottom line:** Always submit Form 12B. A higher monthly TDS now is better than a ₹50K+ tax bill in March.

## Step-by-Step Checklist

### Before Leaving Current Job

- [ ] **Get Form 16 (now Form 130)** — Your salary certificate. New employer needs this.
- [ ] **Get relieving letter** — Confirms your last working day
- [ ] **Download payslips** — All payslips from April to exit month
- [ ] **Note your PF account number** — For EPF transfer
- [ ] **Check gratuity/leave encashment** — Taxable components of full & final settlement

### Day 1 at New Job

- [ ] **Submit Form 12B (Form 124)** — Give new employer your previous salary details
- [ ] **Submit investment declarations** — So TDS is calculated correctly from month 1
- [ ] **Request employer NPS setup** — Ask for 14% of Basic
- [ ] **Opt for meal vouchers** — ₹200/meal under new 2026 rules

### Within First Month

- [ ] **Transfer EPF** — Use EPFO portal to transfer old PF to new account
- [ ] **Verify first payslip** — Check that TDS reflects combined income
- [ ] **Start Sodexo/meal card** — If opted for meal vouchers

## TDS Calculation with Two Jobs

Here's how your TDS should work for a mid-year switch (April–July old job, August–March new job):

```
Old job gross (4 months):     ₹3,81,200
New job gross (8 months):     ₹15,40,000
Combined gross:               ₹19,21,200
Standard deduction:           -    75,000
Taxable income:               ₹18,46,200
Total tax (new regime):        ₹1,76,010

TDS already deducted by old employer: ~₹12,000
Remaining TDS needed:                 ₹1,64,010
Monthly TDS at new job:               ₹1,64,010 ÷ 8 = ~₹20,500
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **Not submitting Form 12B** — leads to under-deduction and year-end shock
2. **Forgetting variable pay** — if you received a bonus/variable from old employer, it counts
3. **Double-claiming 80C** — EPF from both jobs counts; don't over-invest in ELSS
4. **Wrong regime choice** — you can choose different regimes for old and new employer's TDS, but you file one ITR with one regime

## Full & Final Settlement Tax

Your last employer pays a "full & final" that may include:
- **Leave encashment** — Taxable for private sector employees
- **Gratuity** — Exempt up to ₹20L if employed ≥5 years
- **Notice period recovery** — Reduces your taxable salary
- **Variable pay** — Fully taxable
