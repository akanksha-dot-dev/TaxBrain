## Overview

Section 80D (now **Section 126** under Income Tax Act 2025) allows deduction for health insurance premiums. Available **only in Old Regime**.

## Deduction Limits

| For Whom | Non-Senior (<60) | Senior (≥60) |
|---|---|---|
| **Self + Family** | ₹25,000 | ₹50,000 |
| **Parents** | ₹25,000 | ₹50,000 |
| **Preventive Checkup** | ₹5,000 (within above limits) | ₹5,000 |

### Maximum Possible Deduction

| Scenario | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Self <60, Parents <60 | ₹50,000 (₹25K + ₹25K) |
| Self <60, Parents ≥60 | ₹75,000 (₹25K + ₹50K) |
| Self ≥60, Parents ≥60 | ₹1,00,000 (₹50K + ₹50K) |

## What Qualifies

- ✅ Health insurance premium (for self, spouse, children, parents)
- ✅ Preventive health checkup (up to ₹5,000 — within the overall limit, not extra)
- ✅ Medical expenditure for senior citizens without insurance
- ❌ Group insurance premium paid by employer (doesn't count — employer pays it)
- ❌ Life insurance premium (that's 80C, not 80D)

## Should You Buy Personal Health Insurance?

**Yes, even if your employer provides group insurance.**

| Factor | Employer Group Insurance | Personal Health Insurance |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Varies (₹3L–₹10L) | You choose (₹5L–₹1Cr) |
| When you leave the job | Coverage ends immediately | Continues |
| Tax benefit (80D) | No — employer pays | Yes — you pay, you claim |
| Pre-existing conditions | Usually covered from day 1 | 2-4 year waiting period |
| Family coverage | Depends on employer | Your choice |

> **Key insight for new regime users:** Even though 80D deduction isn't available in the new regime, buying health insurance is still essential for **financial protection**. Don't skip it just because there's no tax benefit.

## Pro Tips

1. **Pay parents' premium** — If your parents don't have insurance, buy them a policy and claim 80D deduction
2. **Get a preventive checkup** — Even a ₹2,000 annual checkup counts within the limit
3. **Pay by digital mode** — Cash payments don't qualify for 80D deduction (except for preventive checkup up to ₹5,000)
