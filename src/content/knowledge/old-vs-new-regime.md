## The Two Regimes

India offers two parallel income tax structures. You must choose one each year (salaried employees choose when filing ITR; it's not locked to employer).

### New Regime (Default since FY 2023-24)

The **new regime** is simpler: lower slab rates, fewer deductions. If you don't opt out, this applies automatically.

| Slab | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | 0% |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

**Standard deduction:** ₹75,000 (increased from ₹50,000 in Budget 2024).

**Rebate under Section 87A:** Full tax rebate if taxable income ≤ ₹12,00,000 (effective zero tax up to ~₹12.75L gross with standard deduction).

### Old Regime

Higher slab rates but allows all deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, home loan, etc.):

| Slab | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | 0% |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

**Standard deduction:** ₹50,000.

## When Does Old Regime Win?

Old regime becomes better **only** when your total deductions + exemptions are large enough to offset the higher slab rates. In practice, this requires:

1. **High rent** (for HRA exemption) — typically ₹25,000+/month in a non-metro city
2. **Home loan** (Section 24b interest up to ₹2L)
3. **Maxed 80C** (₹1.5L in EPF + PPF + ELSS)
4. **Health insurance** (80D)
5. **NPS** (80CCD(1B) additional ₹50K)

**Rule of thumb:** If your total deductions exceed ~₹3.75L, old regime *might* be better. Use the [Calculator](/calculator) to know for certain.

## Key Differences

| Feature | New Regime | Old Regime |
|---|---|---|
| Standard Deduction | ₹75,000 | ₹50,000 |
| Section 80C (₹1.5L) | ❌ | ✅ |
| Section 80D (Health) | ❌ | ✅ |
| HRA Exemption | ❌ | ✅ |
| Home Loan Interest | ❌ | ✅ |
| LTA | ❌ | ✅ |
| Employer NPS (80CCD(2)) | ✅ 14% | ✅ 10% |
| Rebate (87A) | ≤₹12L | ≤₹5L |

## The Employer NPS Advantage

One of the **few deductions available in BOTH regimes** is employer NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2). But the limits differ:

- **New Regime:** 14% of Basic salary (since Budget 2024)
- **Old Regime:** 10% of Basic salary (private sector)

This makes employer NPS the single most valuable optimization in the new regime.

## Our Recommendation

For most salaried professionals earning ₹10L–₹30L without a home loan:

> **Choose the New Regime** + maximize employer NPS (14%) + meal vouchers.

The math usually favors new regime unless you have a home loan with significant interest payments.
