## Overview

Section 80C (renamed to **Section 123** under Income Tax Act 2025) allows a deduction of up to **₹1,50,000** per financial year. This is the most widely used tax-saving section — but it's available **only in the Old Regime**.

> **New Regime users:** 80C deductions are NOT available. Your EPF contributions still happen but don't give you a tax benefit. This is one reason the new regime has lower slab rates instead.

## Eligible Investments

| Investment | Lock-in | Returns | Risk | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EPF** (Employee Provident Fund) | Till retirement | ~8.25% (tax-free) | Very Low | Salaried employees (automatic) |
| **PPF** (Public Provident Fund) | 15 years | ~7.1% (tax-free) | Zero | Ultra-safe, long-term savings |
| **ELSS** (Equity Linked Savings) | 3 years | 10-15% (market) | Moderate-High | Best returns among 80C options |
| **NPS** (National Pension System) | Till 60 | 8-12% (market) | Moderate | Additional ₹50K under 80CCD(1B) |
| **NSC** (National Savings Certificate) | 5 years | ~7.7% (taxable) | Zero | Fixed income preference |
| **SCSS** (Senior Citizen Savings) | 5 years | ~8.2% | Zero | Senior citizens only |
| **Tax-Saving FD** | 5 years | ~7% (taxable) | Zero | Simple, familiar |
| **LIC Premium** | Varies | 4-6% | Zero | If already holding policies |
| **Sukanya Samriddhi** | Till daughter turns 21 | ~8% (tax-free) | Zero | Parents of girl children |

## Eligible Expenses (Also Count Under 80C)

These are **not investments** but still qualify:

- **Tuition fees** — Up to 2 children, full-time education only (not coaching/tuition classes)
- **Home loan principal repayment** — EMI principal component
- **Stamp duty & registration charges** — Year of purchase only

## EPF: Your Auto-80C

If you're salaried, your **EPF contribution is automatically deducted** (12% of Basic). For many people, this alone eats up a large chunk of the ₹1.5L limit:

```
Basic salary: ₹1,10,000/month
Annual EPF: ₹1,10,000 × 12% × 12 = ₹1,58,400
```

In this example, EPF alone **exceeds the ₹1.5L limit**. Additional 80C investments give zero extra benefit.

> **Pro tip:** Check your EPF contribution before buying ELSS or tax-saving FDs. Many people over-invest in 80C because they forget EPF already counts.

## ELSS vs PPF vs NPS

| Factor | ELSS | PPF | NPS |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lock-in** | 3 years | 15 years | Till 60 |
| **Returns** | Market-linked (10-15%) | 7.1% fixed | Market-linked (8-12%) |
| **Tax on gains** | LTCG >₹1.25L taxed at 12.5% | Fully exempt | Partial tax at withdrawal |
| **80C benefit** | ✅ Within ₹1.5L | ✅ Within ₹1.5L | ✅ 80CCD(1) within ₹1.5L |
| **Extra benefit** | None | None | Additional ₹50K under 80CCD(1B) |
| **Liquidity** | After 3 years | Partial from year 7 | Only at 60 |

**For first-time investors:** ELSS is the best 80C option — shortest lock-in, highest potential returns, and you can start SIPs with as little as ₹500/month.

## Strategy for Job Switchers

When you switch jobs mid-year:

1. **EPF from both jobs counts** — add up both employers' EPF deductions
2. **Don't double-invest** — calculate total EPF first, then fill the gap with ELSS/PPF
3. **Submit proof to new employer** — provide investment declarations (Form 12BB / now Form 124) to avoid excess TDS
